The boat

The same raised saloon as Paragon, with a very different life.

Eden is a Wauquiez Pilot Saloon 47, hull 003, HIN WAU470031505, USCG official number 1179844, documented for pleasure. Her home and hailing port is Seward, Alaska, and she lives in the Seward Small Boat Harbor. She was built in 2005. The family commissioned her for the 2006 Alaska season, which is why the project files called her a 2006 until the survey arrived. Berret-Racoupeau drew the hull. It is the same design as Paragon.

She is a private sale. There is no YachtWorld, Boat Trader, or SailboatListings page. The ask is $299,000, and the owner said on the phone that there is room to negotiate.

Nikki found her through an old Facebook Marketplace post, about three years stale and no longer public. The owner’s story, as told to us: the family bought a PS40 at the Seattle boat show, traded it when the PS47 launched, flew to France in 2005 to watch this hull come out of the mold, specified the interior, showed her at Annapolis and Seattle, commissioned her at CSR Marine in Seattle for electronics, heat, and systems, had sails made by Schattauer in Seattle, then took her north for summer 2006. One family from the mold.

Her season is mid-May to October. The rest of the year she is hauled and winterized. The owner says there have been no groundings or ice on Eden. The family’s earlier PS40 hit a rock. He says the boat is for sale partly because his insurer was leaving the business and wanted a survey on a twenty-year-old boat, which is hard to get done in Seward.

What is already aboard

Webasto diesel hydronic heat through the boat plus a forced-air unit. The owner says seven zones and a backup, and that the Webasto was rebuilt with a spare aboard. The larger 172-gallon fuel tank. A Northern Lights genset at 728.6 hours, 6 kW per the owner. Harken electric winches. A Lewmar electric and manual windlass, refurbished in 2025 per the owner. A bow thruster. Dual wheels and a full enclosure. A new AGM house bank in spring 2026, per the owner. A custom stainless bracket for a windvane.

What the file leaves open

The standing rig is the original 2005 wire. The teak deck is aging and its outside edges are starting to peel. The survey names a small separation at the aft keel-to-hull joint. The engine’s major service was planned and pushed back. No watermaker, no solar, no vane, and no life raft. Electronics are the 2005–06 Raymarine E120 package. Draft has never been taped. Hours and maintenance are not logged, and the meter photo we asked for has not arrived.

Marathon Marine Surveyor · file MMS.009.2026

An insurance survey, rated Average, with three findings.

Rex von Borstel of Marathon Marine Surveyor LLC inspected Eden in and out of the water at Seward Small Boat Harbor on 26 April 2026. The report is dated 2 May 2026 and runs 70 pages. It was prepared for the owner as a condition and value survey for reinsurance. It is not a pre-purchase survey.

The report says what it did not do. No sea trial. The HIN was taken from the papers, not verified on the hull. Engine internals were not evaluated, and the report recommends a qualified engine surveyor. Electronics were checked for power-up only. Nothing was removed or opened. Moisture was a Tramex Skipper walk-around that read zero. There is no map and there are no core plugs. On a twenty-year-old balsa-cored boat in an Alaska harbor, that is a screen, not a dry core. Almost every system line in the body reads “visually inspected and found to be in good condition.”

Fair market value $338,253. Replacement cost $650,000. The value is the average of two asking prices: a 2005 PS47 in Italy at $281,505 and a 2015 boat in the Seychelles at $395,000.

That is not a West Coast sold-boat set. It is useful as the number the owner’s insurer was willing to write. The ask sits under it.

The three findings, in the surveyor’s words

1. Keel. “The wing keel has a small separation located in the aft section up against the hull. This could be caused by keel bolts being lose or cavitation.” The body of the same report calls the keel and the keel bolts good. This is the only structural sentence in the report, and it is the one that has to be explained before a number.

2. Rudder. “The rudder thru-hull has signs of cavitation on the hull; there is a small section of hull paint missing near the support ring.” Usually a haul inspection of the bearing and packing, not a new rudder, unless a purchase surveyor finds play.

3. Teak. “The teak deck is showing signs of aging; recommend during the next haul out the deck plate be refurbished.” The body still says the planking is in good condition. The owner says the outside edges are starting to peel up.

The summary closes: “vessel has been cared for and is in no need of major repairs.” That is the insurer’s sentence. It is not ours until the keel joint is explained.

Webasto

Heat, the bright spot

The survey lists a Webasto forced-air diesel heater and hydronic units throughout the boat. The owner says seven zones and a backup, and that it rages. The 21 September call notes add that it was rebuilt and a spare is aboard. No air conditioning. This is the system Paragon does not have.

0 W

Energy, short of the brief

12 volt. Four Blue Sea house batteries and two start batteries in two banks. Charles 100 A charger. Xantrex Prosine 3.0 inverter. The owner says the AGM bank is new in spring 2026. Its capacity is not stated. No solar is listed anywhere. Shore power is 120 V, 35 A, and the galvanic isolator aboard does not convert 230 V 50 Hz.

172 gal

Fuel, the larger tank

One plastic tank, starboard midships, 172 US gallons. Water is one 162-gallon tank in the forward V-berth. Holding is one plastic tank, estimated at 40 gallons. The larger fuel tank is what makes the 472-mile Seward to Sitka hop less frightening. It does not make a fall crossing sane.

Rig and sails

  • Single mast with boom, two spreaders. Shrouds, stays, turnbuckles, toggles, and chainplates visually “good” from deck. It is the original 2005 wire. Replace before an ocean.
  • Survey sail note: I 63.08 ft, J 17.15 ft, E 21.0 ft, foretriangle 540.91 sq ft, total 1,131.54 sq ft, forestay about 65.37 ft. The P figure is misprinted in the report.
  • The survey saw one sail, “visible and mounted.” The owner describes a Schattauer main, a roller-furling headsail, and an asymmetric with a snuffer, at about 90%. No loft card, no year.
  • Harken electric winches. Running rigging, blocks, traveller, and reefing visually good.
  • The owner says a rigger would have to come up from Seattle. Seward has no yacht rigger or sail loft.

Helm, cockpit, deck

  • Teak decks over molded fiberglass, teak in the cockpit, cap-type hull-to-deck joint with a teak overlay. Body text says good. Summary says aging. The owner says the outside edges are peeling up and need work.
  • Two wheels, manual cable steering, emergency tiller stowed aft. No rudder position indicator.
  • Full enclosure and covers by Sam’s Custom Sewing in Seward. Hatches, portlights, and windows visually good, no water intrusion seen.
  • Lewmar manual and electric windlass, refurbished in 2025 per the owner. The survey says a shovel-type anchor and galvanized BBB chain. The 21 September notes say a roughly 55 lb anchor of unknown make, 100 ft of chain, and 500 ft of rope.
  • Stainless bowsprit channel bolted to the bow. A small davit on the starboard quarter for the outboard. A custom stainless windvane bracket, brand unknown, with no vane on it.
  • Tender is not settled. The survey lists a small Avon and a Yamaha 15 hp. The 21 September notes list a 10 ft 6 in Highfield of about three years and a Honda 10 hp of about ten.

Navigation and power

  • Raymarine E120 for radar, GPS, plotter, speed, and depth. Raymarine Tridata and Graphic displays. Icom IC-M502 VHF. This is the 2005–06 CSR commissioning package. AIS is not listed.
  • ACR 406 MHz EPIRB. No life raft aboard. Shaft zinc was not replaced at the April haul. The prop zinc was.
  • Northern Lights genset, three cylinders, 728.6 indicated hours, in the midships bilge on its own Racor R24S.

Engine and water

  • Yanmar 4JH3-HTE, serial E23705, four cylinders. 2,789.7 indicated hours on 26 April 2026. The survey prints 110 hp. The project brief notes the factory 4JH3-HTE is 100 hp turbo.
  • The owner first wrote that he thought the engine had 7,000 hours. On the 21 September call he said hours and maintenance are not logged, and that oil and belts are done each year. The major overhaul planned for summer was pushed back. He says the engine runs great, with no smoke.
  • Mounts, hoses, belts, strainers, and wet exhaust visually good. Racor fuel filtering. Cable controls. Transmission maker not identified.
  • No watermaker. Kuuma 6-gallon electric water heater, with a heat exchanger from the main engine. Two manual heads with a Type III MSD and a macerator. Two LPG bottles in a stern locker for a three-burner stove and oven.

Hull

  • Molded fiberglass, gelcoat finish. Bilges clean. No blisters, no borers. Bottom paint still active at the April 2026 haul.
  • The factory build is vacuum-infused balsa sandwich in hull and deck. Twenty freeze-thaw seasons and a peeling teak overlay are the reason “no moisture measured” is not a cleared core.
  • The aft keel-to-hull separation, the rudder-tube cavitation, and the depth of the keel are purchase-survey items.

One informal ask, one insurance number, one coach’s band

$299,000 is a conversation, not a listing.

On the 12 September phone call the owner said $299,000 stands, with room for negotiation. He confirmed the same on 21 September. There is no broker and no public page. His insurer will write her at $338,253. The ask is under that, which helps, and it does not make $299,000 cheap once rig, teak, keel, and Alaska are paid.

Sailing Totem, on the 23 September call, moved the number down. Their working picture is an easy $150,000 refit if the work is hired at Puget Sound or Sidney rates. Teak replacement is about $40,000 in the US. The standing rig is $16,000 to $18,000 unless turnbuckles and hardware stack on. Energy, meaning lithium, solar, and maybe an arch, is $35,000 to $40,000 quickly. The keel gap is unknown until someone opens it: torque the nuts, or a rock stretched the bolts, or embedded bolts that mean a real yard job. Even $250,000 is not enough in their view, given how little is documented. Low $200s is the band where they would keep the process going.

The planning close in the calculator is $250,000, the field in the all-in workbook. The workbook also records that the saved .tru note said $275,000. Neither is an offer.

Three ways to read the file

Totem’s verdict. Do not fly to Seward at $299,000. Decide as a family whether the owner will entertain the low $200s. If not, let Eden go and walk Paragon if the PS47 is still the design.

The brief’s honest range. If the core and the keel are clean, the September brief put her all-in at about $340,000 on the low side and about $420,000 on the honest side, from a mid-$200s to $299,000 close.

The park case. A $299,000 as-is close, a teak recore, and a structural keel joint put her all-in over $450,000.

Before anyone flies to Seward

Ask these before a number.

  1. A phone photo of the hour meter and the engine plate. Is 2,789.7 the original meter? Reconcile it with “I think 7,000.”
  2. The surveyor’s photo of the keel-to-hull separation, and a tape on the draft. “Deep” and “wing” cannot both be left hanging.
  3. Groundings in writing. The owner says none on Eden.
  4. Teak plugs, seams, and leaks under the peeling edges.
  5. The battery story: what was replaced in spring 2026, and at what amp-hours.
  6. The anchor’s make, the chain length, and the vane bracket’s brand.
  7. The Schattauer loft card, and the year of each sail.
  8. The full list of work done in Seattle, and whether the major engine service happens before a close.
  9. Which tender is aboard: the Avon and Yamaha in the survey, or the Highfield and Honda in the call notes.
  10. If the conversation continues, the ceiling letter Totem described: we love the boat, here are teak, rig, energy, and keel with quotes, this is our number, call us if you can meet it.

How to read the range

The low path is a $250,000 close, tax at zero, and the low end of every line. It lands at $365,750, under the $400,000 cap and over the $350,000 floor of the band. It already carries $15,000 for the standing rig, $9,000 for a second pilot, $9,000 for an inner forestay, and $10,000 of contingency.

The high path at the same close is $484,150. Teak at $18,000, the keel joint at $15,000, rig, forestay, and nav at the top of their lines do not fit under the cap. Totem’s $150,000 hired refit sits between the two columns. The keel joint and the teak core decide which column is true.

Private sale · Seward

The owner, not a broker.

Eden is sold by the family that ordered her. There is no listing broker and no public page. The conversation has been personal on purpose, and no hard negotiation has opened. The owner was sailing in Scotland in mid-September 2026, so a full listing waits until he is home. The boat stays in Seward. The project has a broker it could introduce, and has not.

The next honest look is a spring 2027 haul in Seward with a surveyor we hire. No deposit, no bid, and no fall 2026 Gulf of Alaska crossing. Seward has a 330-ton city lift for a survey haul. Authorized Yanmar support is in Anchorage. Rig, teak, and systems work would wait for Port Townsend or Bellingham.
