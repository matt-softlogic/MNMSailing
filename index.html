Nikki & Matt · the years, not a brochure

Pick the boat that can keep this calendar.

The route below is the August 2026 sailing timeline, drawn as the test these four boats have to pass. The written boat is a capable 45 to 55 foot bluewater monohull. Turnkey, or close to it. Professional work, not a two-year owner refit. In the water and shaking down in time for a 2028 departure south, and a 2029 window across the Pacific. Sara Lynn is still for sale and is longer than that test, so she is filed under Maybes. Mariana and Second Wind are still for sale and are filed there too.

Foundation · now through late 2026

Clear the deck.

Sell the boat we already have. November is a Sailing Totem consult, and Matt crews the Baja Ha-Ha. The search starts when sale proceeds are actually in hand, which can be late 2026 or the start of 2027.

2027

Define and align · January through May

Agree the boat before agreeing the name.

January locks the budget and the turnkey list. March is a week together on a Baja charter, so the route is a shared decision and not a spreadsheet. May is the co-ownership agreement. The cash ceiling used on each study is $400,000 all-in. Commissioning has to come out from under the ask.

02
2027

Acquire and prepare · into early 2028

Buy her where we can walk her, then get the work done.

Priority is a boat already in the Northwest, with a working yard we trust. For a Washington purchase the plan is a dated exit and the first real yard period in Sidney, not a winter parked as a Washington liveaboard. A southern boat can still win, if we accept that the route then starts in California or that she has to be delivered north.

03
2028

Shake-down

Islands, then south.

Spring in the San Juans and British Columbia. June is the year for Van Isle 360 if the boat and the calendar agree. A light yard period in summer. August southbound. September and October into Mexico. October and November in the Sea of Cortez. December at La Ventana or Cabo, with friends and family.

04
2029

The ocean

Panama, then the Pacific window.

January, six to eight weeks south toward Panama. February through April is the crossing window to New Zealand, by way of the South Pacific islands. After landfall, coastal New Zealand. The boat that cannot carry fuel, heat, a vane, and a watermaker into that window is the wrong boat, however pretty the saloon.

Same formulas · nine hulls

Where the design ratios sit.

These are the scales Sailing Totem prints for waterline, beam, displacement, sail area, and comfort. The color is the band on that scale. It means more of that quality. It is not a grade. A higher comfort number and a higher sail-area number describe different boats. Real Feel, the roomy ratio, and Kerr speed are their own indexes. The formulas are not published, so those three are not drawn here. Capsize screening is the ordinary companion to comfort, with the old 2.0 line marked.
