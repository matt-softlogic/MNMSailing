The boat

The gear is bought. The hours are not.

Mariana is a 2001 Moody 47, shoal keel, lying at Royal River Boat Yard in Yarmouth, Maine. Bill Dixon drew this model. Princess Yachts built it from August 2001 to October 2004, 46 hulls. She is a cutter: in-mast main, yankee, staysail, and an asymmetric on a bowsprit. USCG official number 1326672, as printed on the seller’s page. No broker is named there. The voice of the listing is the owner’s.

The ask is $220,000. The specification says the engine has about 7,500 hours. A few pages earlier the same document says roughly 7,600.

The current family says they sailed about 4,000 miles on her, a year of living aboard, from the British Virgin Islands through the Bahamas and up the East Coast, and that they have settled in Maine. The same page says the boat herself has done tens of thousands of miles, including an Atlantic crossing, and that two families have lived aboard for years. Those sentences can both be true. They are not the same number, and the earlier miles are not itemized.

She is laid out as a four-cabin, seven-berth, two-head boat. The forward head is a Raritan electric. The aft head is a Jabsco, rebuilt in 2025. The seller is plain about one scar: the original teak decks leaked, the interior veneer shows it, and the price is set to reflect that cosmetic damage. The teak itself was replaced with Dek-King in 2016. “Cosmetic” is the seller’s word. A survey has to say whether the core stayed dry.

What is already paid for

Dek-King decks, 2016. Standing rigging, January 2016, inspected with the chainplates in 2025. Coppercoat 2018, refreshed 2023. Yankee 2016, repaired 2025. In-mast main 2013. Asymmetric, furler, and bowsprit 2019. 600 Ah lithium. 300 W solar, a Rutland 1200, and a Whisper Power 3.5 kW generator. Spectra 380C. Eberspächer heat. Lewmar Ocean windlass rebuilt 2026. Turbo and exhaust flex 2026. Shaft and PSS 2019. MaxProp rebuilt by PYI 2022. Highfield 310 from 2019.

What the listing leaves open

The hour figure is printed twice and does not match. The lithium year is not given. Generator hours, watermaker membrane age, and life-raft service dates are not given. The staysail is described as original. Solar is 300 watts against a 600 Ah bank. There is no windvane. The AC system is 230 volts and 50 hertz. The old deck leak is disclosed, and then called cosmetic.

Factory card · and the class sheet that borrows a waterline

Use the shoal weight. Do not borrow the old boat.

Sailboatdata’s “Moody 47” is a different Dixon boat, built 1982 to 1989, hull length 46 ft 6 in, Thornycroft auxiliary. This Mariana is the later Moody 47, introduced August 2001. The factory card prints LOA 47 ft 8 in including the pulpit, hull length 46 ft 6 in, beam 14 ft 5 in, fin draft 6 ft 9 in, shoal draft 5 ft 3 in. Fin displacement is 32,890 lb. The second displacement on that card is 33,726 lb, which is the number on this listing, and it matches the shoal boat. Ballast is 10,340 lb on both. Listings Port’s Moody 47-2 sheet uses the fin weight and a 38.75 ft waterline. The factory card never prints a waterline, and 38.75 ft is also the waterline of the 1980s boat. The ratios below say which number they used.

259

Displacement / length

Listing weight, 33,726 lb, on the 38.75 ft waterline Listings Port publishes. That is about 259. The same waterline on the fin weight, 32,890 lb, is the sheet’s 252. If the “extended waterline” in the brochure is longer than 38.75 ft, both numbers are heavy. The brochure does not give the length that would correct them.

14.4

Sail area / displacement

Foretriangle plus main triangle, from the factory I, J, P, and E, is about 942 sq ft. On this hull’s 33,726 lb that is a sail-area ratio near 14.4. The cutter’s extra cloth, a 565 sq ft yankee plus a 210 sq ft staysail, is not added on top of that triangle. The in-mast main is published at 441 sq ft, which is the triangle, not a full-batten sail.

30.7%

Ballast ratio

10,340 lb of ballast on 33,726 lb. The fin-keel class figure is 31.4% only because that hull is listed lighter and the ballast number does not change. Lead is what the class sheet says. The seller’s page does not repeat the metal.

1.78

Capsize screening

Beam 14 ft 5 in on the listing displacement. The fin-weight class figure is about 1.80. Under 2 is the usual offshore screen. It is a screen, not a stability booklet. No AVS is published here.

35.4

Comfort, as published

Listings Port prints 35.41 for the fin-keel sheet. That uses their waterline and the lighter displacement. It is not recomputed here for the shoal hull, because the waterline is the soft number.

8.34 kn

Hull speed, on their waterline

1.34 times the square root of 38.75 ft. A longer real waterline would raise it. Fuel is 114 gallons, the same short tank the factory printed. Water is the generous one: 204 gallons.

A British boat in Maine

The AC system is listed as 230 volts, 50 hertz. That is a gift if she later sits on a New Zealand pontoon, and a problem in a U.S. marina until someone maps the inverter, the shore cord, and the transformer. Do not assume the cord in the locker matches the pedestal.

The ask, and what it is not

$220,000 is the whole advertisement.

There is no sold-boat history in this file, and no twelve-month class median worth pretending is hers. A different Moody 47, a 2002 in Palma, has been advertised near $347,000 with about 3,500 hours. That boat is not this boat, and her length on that ad is the shorter hull. Mariana’s ask is low against that kind of number because the seller has already said the interior shows the old leak, and because 7,500 hours is not 3,500 hours.

The planning closes used below are $200,000, $210,000, and the $220,000 ask. They are a way to see the reserve. They are not an offer, and nobody on the other side of this listing has agreed to them.

She does not sit on the route. Pegasus is already in San Diego. Paragon is in Anacortes. Mariana is in Maine. Choosing her means the first year is an East Coast exit, or a delivery that is not priced anywhere in the packet. The delivery line in the calculator is zero until a number exists.

Where she sits on the calendar

Phase 2 on the home page is a boat you can walk, then work, then shake down in the islands. Mariana can be walked in Yarmouth. The work is a diesel specialist and a moisture map, not a new interior. The shake-down is not the San Juans unless someone moves her there. She is the boat whose ask leaves room for that work, and whose engine can spend the room.

TRU all-in method · editable

What she costs to commission, not just to buy.

Price, purchase costs, then a low and a high reserve. Labor is priced as hired. Insurance defaults to 2% of the price in use. Tax defaults to zero. Maine’s statewide sales and use tax is 5.5%, with no local sales tax on top, and a nonresident who buys a watercraft and removes it can fall under an exemption in Maine statute. The switch puts 5.5% in. It does not decide whether the exemption applies. This is planning arithmetic, not tax advice, and not an offer.

Before a number

Ask for the paper the packet promises.

  1. The hour meter and the engine plate, photographed on the day. Is it about 7,500, or roughly 7,600, or something else?
  2. The oil-analysis report the seller says shows healthy wear. The trend, not a sentence.
  3. The 2016 Dek-King invoice, and whether the old teak came off to the substrate or was covered.
  4. The 2025 rig inspection, including the chainplates, in the inspector’s words.
  5. Generator hours, model, and a load test. Watermaker membrane date and a jug of product water.
  6. Lithium make, install date, and a drawing of the ProMariner, the Victron DC-DC, and the Dakar.
  7. Life-raft certificate and both EPIRB service dates.
  8. A diesel mechanic on a cold start, not only a general surveyor with a camera.

How to read the range

The low path is a $200,000 close, tax at zero, and the small end of every reserve. It still lands near $248,000 because the engine reserve and the contingency are in the low column on purpose. A clean survey can shrink the deck line. It should not erase the hours.

The high path, at the ask, approaches the planning cap before anyone has bought a delivery west. If the core is wet or the Volvo is finished, the ask stops being the interesting number.
