The boat

Three owners, one ask, and a saloon you can see out of.

Paragon is a 2008 Wauquiez Pilot Saloon 47, hull 34, offered in Anacortes by Seattle Yachts. Berret-Racoupeau drew the hull. Wauquiez built it in France, infused glass over balsa, vinylester in the outer skins, CE Category A. The point of the boat is the raised saloon: you sit up, you see the horizon, and the owner’s cabin is still aft of an aft cockpit.

The ask is $379,000. It was $379,000 in April 2024, and the print of that listing is stamped sold on 24 February 2025 at the same number.

The first owner, in Annapolis, has said publicly that hull 34 did about 40,000 miles, East Coast and then California, and that she never showed a structural or design fault. Sold in February 2021. The second chapter is California and Mexico: a 2024 packet places her at Paradise Village, Puerto Vallarta, LLC-owned, heading for San Diego, with a priced 2019–2024 refit the packet put near $85,000. The third chapter is the current one. The broker’s paragraph says the present owner bought her and sailed her up the coast to Anacortes. No 2025 or 2026 work appears on the public list. The hours still read the way they read in April 2024.

She sleeps the usual three-cabin plan: an owner’s cabin aft, a guest cabin forward, a Pullman amidships, two heads. USCG official number 1219300, name PARAGON, recreational, United States flag, built 2008, status Active on a PSIX snapshot from 9 September 2026. Owner names are not in that public record.

What is already paid for

Hydrovane and a tiller pilot from 2022. Rainman watermaker from 2021. Quantum HydraNet main and genoa from 2019, plus a furling asymmetric. B&G offshore package from November 2020. Rocna 33 kg with extra chain, and a Fortress FX-23. Three electric winches, a bow thruster, Kato davits. Max Prop and a PSS shaft seal from 2021. Frigoboat keel cooler from 2024. Lifeline 800 Ah from November 2023. Enclosure, dodger, and bimini from 2023. A Northern Lights genset advertised at 244 hours.

What the listing leaves open

The engine line says 530 hours reported, and has said that since at least April 2024. Other copies of the ad say 770, and one listing photograph of the meter looks like the mid-700s. Standing rigging has no invoice, so it is 2008 wire until a receipt appears. Teak was sanded and varnished in 2023. Heat is reverse-cycle, not diesel hydronic. Solar is 525 watts. Fuel is the factory 104 gallons. The tender’s identity changes from page to page.

Class design · Sailboatdata, and this hull’s keel block

A moderate hull that still wants to sail.

The ratios below are the published class sheet: 30,864 lb on a 41.83 ft waterline, sail area 1,132 sq ft. They describe the boat Sailboatdata prints, whose max-draft line is 6.89 ft, with a note that the shallow keel is 5.91 ft. Paragon is that shallow boat. Her listing also prints a heavier displacement, 33,296 lb, and a heavier ballast, 9,982 lb, against a factory shoal ballast often cited near 8,995 lb. Use the class ratios to understand the hull. Use the listing numbers to understand this keel.

188

Displacement / length

On the class weight. A moderate cruising hull, in the same band as a lot of performance cruisers and well under a heavy full-keel boat. At the listing’s 33,296 lb the same waterline moves the ratio up toward about 203.

18.5

Sail area / displacement

1,132 sq ft on the class displacement. That is a boat that will sail in light air, which is what the first owner of this hull said she did. The heavier listing weight would pull the ratio down toward about 17.5.

28.9%

Ballast ratio

Class sheet, 8,931 lb of lead in 30,864 lb. The listing’s own pair, 9,982 lb in 33,296 lb, is about 30%. Either way this is a bulb keel, not a wing full of lead.

1.89

Capsize screening

Beam 14.8 ft on the class sheet, over the cube root of that displacement. Under the common screen of 2.0. The listing beam is 15 ft 2 in. Confirm which stick and which beam are on the hull.

30.4

Comfort ratio

Sailboatdata’s 30.38 for the class weight. Brewer’s mid range: a coastal and offshore cruiser, not a motorsailer and not a light racer.

House bank, still AGM

Lifeline batteries, November 2023, 800 amp-hours. An Xantrex 3000 inverter/charger of the same date. A 24-volt bank for the thruster, and separate start batteries. Enough to live aboard for a season. Not the lithium plant a Pacific year wants, and not something that has to be bought on day one if the Lifelines load-test.

525 W

Solar, short of the brief

Three Renogy 175 W panels from 2021 on a Blue Sky controller. The route’s energy note is closer to 1,000 or 1,200 watts, plus a Victron bus and an isolation transformer so a New Zealand marina at 230 volts and 50 hertz has somewhere to go. The galvanic isolator already aboard does not do that job.

104 gal

Fuel, and no spare tank on the list

Factory fuel on this listing is 104 gallons. Water is 211. Holding, on the 2024 print, is 30. The class sheet on Sailboatdata prints 172 gallons of fuel and 162 of water. This hull is not that tank plan. A long hop has to be planned around 104 gallons, or around a bladder that is not on the public list.

Rig and sails

  • Fractional sloop. In-mast furling main, furling genoa, furling asymmetric. Listing rig: I 63.08 ft, J 17.17 ft, P 56.25 ft, E 21.00 ft.
  • Quantum HydraNet radial main and genoa, 2019. Adjustable genoa car leads. Three electric winches.
  • Standing rigging age is not on the list. Until a receipt shows up, treat the wire as original 2008.
  • Running rigging is a service item, not a dated replacement.

Helm, cockpit, deck

  • Dual wheels. Teak on the side decks, the cockpit seats, and the cockpit sole. Sanded and varnished in 2023. Varnish on a walking surface is a slip, and it can hide a wet seam. Thickness and plugs are a walk item.
  • Dodger, bimini, and a full enclosure, new in 2023. Canvas from the Mexico years. Look at the windows.
  • Hydrovane and a tiller pilot, 2022. Keep them. A second electronic pilot is a preference, not a missing part.
  • Rocna 33 kg with an extra 100 feet of chain. Electric windlass with a chain counter. Fortress FX-23.
  • Kato davits. The tender on them is not a settled fact: a 2021 Highfield, an AB with an 8 hp Nissan, and a Tohatsu from November 2023 all appear in copies of this boat’s ads. Photograph whatever is actually on the davits.
  • Bow thruster. Max feathering prop, PYI PSS shaft seal from 2021, Shaft Shark.

Navigation and power

  • B&G offshore package, November 2020. A camera under the radar, tied to a display. Some 2024 photos still show a mixed Simrad and B&G helm. Look at what is in the pods today.
  • Factory Wauquiez AC/DC panel, labeled in French and English, with three tank switches.
  • Northern Lights genset, advertised at 244 hours. Starter rebuilt in 2022. Start battery the same year. Hours have sat still on paper along with the engine.

Engine, water, heat

  • Yanmar 4JH4-HTE, 110 hp. The current sentence is “530 hours (reported).” A mechanical meter in the listing photographs appears to read about 766. Read it again, on the boat, and ask for the service file.
  • Racor MAX dual filters. An upgraded Yanmar panel with temperature and oil-pressure gauges.
  • Rainman portable watermaker, 2021. Keep it. Do not buy a Spectra on day one.
  • Frigoboat keel cooler, evaporator, and compressor, 2024. Two electric heads. The aft-head thru-hull is dated 2024.
  • Air conditioning and heat, reverse cycle. That is the comfort system on the list. It is not a diesel furnace. A Pacific Northwest winter aboard needs hydronic heat. Budget it, or do not winter aboard.
  • EPIRB listed as needing recertification. Jacklines aboard. A life raft is not clearly on the list.

Hull

  • Shoal bulb keel. The keel block says 5.92 ft, ballast 9,982 lb, displacement 33,296 lb. The Denison header that says 7 ft 9 in is the other factory draft. Ignore the header.
  • Balsa-cored deck and hull topsides, solid or nearly so below, as the class is built. No moisture plugs are on file after Mexico and three owners. Sample on the haul.
  • A 2024 haul-out packet shows a manta-ray painted on the bow and the Frigoboat plate. Ask what else that yard did.

One boat, one price, three harbors

The ask has not been a negotiation.

There is no clean class median to hide behind. What exists is this hull’s own paper. On 24 April 2024 a Yacht Sales West listing, broker Ian Van Tuyl, asked $379,000. The location field said San Diego. The prose said Paradise Village, Puerto Vallarta, heading for San Diego, and an LLC on the title. That print is stamped sold on 24 February 2025 at $379,000.

The current page, Seattle Yachts in Anacortes, Alex Knudtson, Denison last updated 17 August 2026, still asks $379,000. The equipment list and the hour sentence did not grow a 2025 season. A same-price flip is not evidence that $379,000 is what the market clears. It is evidence that nobody has had to move the number yet.

The planning close in the calculator is $340,000. That is a target so the low path can still see a $400,000 all-in ceiling. It is not an offer, and it is not a number a broker has agreed to.

Three ways to read the file

$340,000 and the low column. Rig paperwork is recent, teak is a recaulk, the AGMs stay, the vane is the pilot, the tender on the davits stays. This is the only version near the ceiling.

$340,000 and the middle of every line. Half a new rig, half a teak project, half a lithium bank, half a second pilot. Already past the ceiling.

$379,000 and the high column. Pay the ask, then fund original wire, wet teak, diesel heat, lithium, and a new tender. That is a different purchase.

On the Anacortes dock, with a camera

Ask these before a number.

  1. Hour meter and engine plate, photographed today. Is it 530, or the mid-700s the older copies and the listing photo suggest? Where is the service file?
  2. Standing-rig receipts, or a date from a rigger. If there is no paper, the wire is 2008.
  3. Teak thickness, plugs, and seams. Is the 2023 varnish on the walking surface, or only on trim?
  4. Title out of the 2024 LLC. Was 24 February 2025 a recorded sale, and who is on the document now?
  5. Any work after February 2025. The public list is frozen.
  6. Dinghy and outboard, as they sit, with serial numbers. Do not buy a tender into this file until that photograph exists.
  7. Heat. Confirm there is no hidden diesel furnace. The list says reverse cycle.
  8. Tank stickers. 104 fuel, 211 water, 30 holding. Any bladder or extra fuel tank not on the list?
  9. Life raft and EPIRB certificate, in hand.
  10. Core readings, or an agreement to plug the deck and the keel area on the survey haul.

How to read the range

The ceiling path is a $340,000 close, tax at zero, and the low end of every reserve. It lands essentially on $400,000 because the low column still carries contingency, a rig look, a teak recaulk, and diesel heat. It is not a cheap boat that happens to have a high ask. It is a boat whose ask already is the ceiling, so the discount and the survey have to do the same job.

The high path is a park. Original wire, wet teak, a furnace, lithium, and a new tender do not fit under the cap at either price. A real survey collapses the rig and teak lines. Until then the spread is the decision.
