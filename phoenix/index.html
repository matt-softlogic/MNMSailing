The boat

The newest raised saloon in the file, with the most miles.

Phoenix is a Wauquiez Pilot Saloon 47, the same Berret-Racoupeau design as Paragon and Eden. The listing calls her a 2015. The broker’s 26 August 2026 email calls her a 2016. That needs to be confirmed against her papers. She is the last PS47 built. According to the broker, the original owner talked the builder into one more hull because he believed the 47 was stronger and better suited to offshore work.

Asking $395,000 with BVI Yacht Sales. She is in the Seychelles. The seller has talked about taking her to the Med and, if she has not sold in Europe, on to the Caribbean.

She has one owner and more than 48,000 miles, from Europe to the Pacific, Southeast Asia, and the Indian Ocean. The boat has her own website documenting those travels. Her Med plans for spring 2026 were dropped because of regional travel concerns, so she stayed in the Seychelles.

What is already aboard

Webasto hydronic central heating, 31,000 BTU/hr, with eight radiators. A 100 L/h watermaker and 500 L of fresh water. New Hamma Dyform standing rigging in 2025. An oversized L&S ST50 autopilot plus a full spare unit. A Mastervolt/Victron custom system: LiFePO4 400 A, a 4,000 W inverter, two chargers, 110/230 V and 50/60 Hz shore power through a shore isolation transformer, lightning protection, a Watt&Sea Pod600, and a second Balmar 250 A alternator with an external regulator. 860 L of fuel. Coppercoat over a four-coat epoxy osmosis barrier. A Rocna 33 kg on 125 m of G70 chain, plus a spare Delta. Andersen electric winches and a bow thruster. A full safety kit and a full set of spares.

What the file leaves open

The July 2024 grounding and the structural repair that followed it. About 3,250 engine hours. Teak decks from 2015. The boat’s circuits are 230 V, although shore power takes 110 V and 60 Hz. Solar is removable panels only, listed as two 120 W. Sail ages and the engine service log are not given. The listing gives the draft as 8 ft 0 in, while the repair report gives 2.10 m. Her year, 2015 or 2016, also needs confirming. And she is a long way from Puget Sound.

July 2024 · Anambas Islands, Indonesia

A grounding, a Phuket repair, and a disclosure.

In July 2024 Phoenix grounded in the Anambas Islands, Indonesia. The broker’s notes describe damage to the keel, the hull, and the internal grid. She sailed on to Phuket, where the structural repair was done in 2024–25 under the supervision of a surveyor. The hull repair report in the project Drive is dated 22 June 2025.

The broker disclosed the grounding in his additional information for buyers. The public listing does not mention it. His view, and the repair surveyor’s, is that the grid is now glass-tabbed to the hull and is stronger than it was new. Coppercoat went on after the repair. She then cruised on to Thailand and the Seychelles in 2025 and 2026.

The repair surveyor was working for the owner. A buyer’s surveyor still has to look at the grid, the keel bolts, and the keel-to-hull joint.

Questions for the broker before any travel

  1. The full repair file: invoices, yard, photographs, and the surveyor’s scope.
  2. The insurance claim, and whether the hull insurer will write her again on the same terms.
  3. A keel-bolt re-torque or inspection after the 2025 season.
  4. The draft, taped: 8 ft 0 in on the listing, 2.10 m in the repair report.
  5. The build year on her papers: 2015 on the listing, 2016 in the email.
  6. The engine log behind about 3,250 hours.
  7. Where she is, and where she will be through 2027.

One ask, three sisters

$395,000 for the most boat, the farthest away.

The ask has not moved in about a year. It is the Seychelles boat that Eden’s insurance survey used as one of its two comparables. Against the brief she has three of the four required items: diesel heat, a watermaker, and a standing rig under ten years old. She also carries a full spare autopilot. She is short on solar. Paragon has one of the four, and Eden has none.

She is also the most expensive of the three to bring home. Delivery, travel, and survey add roughly $45,000 to $70,000 to get her to Puget Sound, so the planning all-in runs from about $454,000 to $567,000. That sits above the $400,000 cap on every line.

Before anyone flies

Ask these before a number.

  1. The complete Phuket repair file and the insurance claim.
  2. An independent structural opinion on the grid and keel, or the right to one at survey.
  3. A taped draft, and the keel option she was built with.
  4. The build year on her papers.
  5. The engine log, oil analysis history, and any major work.
  6. Watermaker, Webasto, and autopilot service records. Sail ages and the loft.
  7. A full condition survey, beyond the repair report.
  8. Teak deck condition: seams, plugs, and leaks.
  9. Her itinerary through 2027, and whether a Caribbean handover is realistic.

How to read the range

The low path is the ask, tax at zero, a $25,000 delivery, and the low end of every line. It lands at $453,950. It already carries solar, a light shore-power adaptation, an engine service, and $3,000 of contingency.

The high path at the same price is $566,650. Grid and keel work at $20,000, teak at $20,000, an engine major, a longer delivery, and sails all go to the top. The buyer’s survey of the repair decides which column is true.
