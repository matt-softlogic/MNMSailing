Summary · hull repair report dated 22 June 2025 · Waterline Marine Ltd, Phuket

The 2024 grounding and the Phuket repair, summarized.

This page summarizes the 17-page hull repair report in Phoenix’s project folder. It does not reproduce it. The report was commissioned by the owner, is copyrighted by the surveying firm, and names private parties and registration details that are left off a public page. The report states that it does not assess stability, performance, overall design or structural integrity.

What happened

In July 2024 Phoenix ran aground on rocks near the Anambas Islands, Indonesia. The impact pushed the aft end of the keel upward. The bonded internal hull grid cracked and came away from the hull shell in several places, some bulkheads, floors and furniture shifted, and the outer hull cracked just aft of the keel joint over about 300 mm. The keel’s lower forward section was dented and its upper aft edge distorted; the keel bolts were intact. The rudder was lightly scratched.

The repair, October 2024 to June 2025

She was hauled at Phuket Boat Lagoon, and a local repair yard did the work. The surveyor made eleven inspections, from 9 October 2024 to 12 June 2025.

Vessel details in the report

2015 Wauquiez Pilot Saloon 47. Berret-Racoupeau design. 14.35 m × 4.51 m × 2.10 m (47.08 × 14.80 × 6.89 ft). Displacement 14,000 kg. Tankage fuel 650 L, water 600 L, “as per vessel documentation”. Yanmar 4JH4-HTE, printed at 100 hp. CE Category A, 8 persons / 3,200 kg. These match the factory sheet, not the broker’s 860 L and 500 L.

InspectionWhat the report records
1 · 9 Oct 2024Mast, rudder, keel, engine and fuel tank removed; part of the interior removed. Hull crack aft of the keel joint, grid detached and cracked in many places, some bulkhead tabbing cracked. Mast step free of cracks.
2 · 1 Nov 2024Coatings removed. More cracks and delamination found, including a crack in way of the keel bolt holes. Centre section of the grid cut out; interior ground back to sound laminate.
3 · 13 Dec 2024Interior hull rebuilt with reportedly 5 layers of 400 g and 3 layers of 600 g biaxial glass in epoxy. New grid sections molded and bonded in.
4 · 14 Jan 2025Grid restored to shape and glassed to the hull with reportedly 20 stepped layers of 400 g biaxial in epoxy. Exterior ground out for new laminate.
5 · 29 Jan 2025Grid restoration in final stages.
6–7 · 8 and 18 Feb 2025Exterior relaminated, including 15 layers of 400 g biaxial in epoxy, laid over three days and vacuum bagged. Interior refit under way.
8 · 6 Mar 2025Interior and systems being reinstalled, including the watermaker membranes.
9–10 · 28–29 Apr 2025Hull faired at the keel joint. 20 mm stainless keel-bolt backing plates fitted. Keel’s damaged upper aft section repaired with an inserted stainless plate and faired after fitting.
11 · 12 Jun 2025Repairs complete; Coppercoat applied below the waterline.

The surveyor’s conclusion

The repairs were done professionally and to established fiberglass repair standards. Post-repair, the hull is considered as strong and resilient as the original, and the vessel “Fit For Purpose”. The surveyor’s opinion is that glassing the grid to the hull, where the factory had bonded it with adhesive, makes the hull stronger overall.

What a buyer still needs

The report was prepared for the owner, and it excludes overall structural integrity. A buyer’s surveyor should look at the grid, the keel bolts and backing plates, the keel-to-hull joint, and the rudder and steering, which the broker’s sheet says were redesigned and rebuilt. Ask for the yard invoices and the insurance file. The report’s 2.10 m draft is the factory standard keel. The listing says 8 ft 0 in.
