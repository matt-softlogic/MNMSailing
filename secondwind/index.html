The boat

The 2025 work is done. The passage kit is not.

Second Wind is a 2014 Beneteau Sense 46, lying in Key West, offered at $319,900. Murray Yacht Sales is the central listing, vessel 2842882. Berret Racoupeau drew the hull. Nauta did the interior. Beneteau built her. The class was first built in 2013. HIN BEYDN021I314. Official number 125880. No salesperson is named on the pages used for this study.

The ask is $319,900. The project study uses a planning close of $295,000. That close is not an offer, and it is not a second price on the listing.

She is the two-cabin, two-head layout. The master is forward, with a centerline berth. The guest cabin is to port, with an ensuite shower. The nav station and the dinette are to starboard. The dinette drops to a berth. The settee folds down. Three steps lead from the cockpit into the saloon. The listing calls that the point of the boat.

The hull is white. The equipment list says the draft is 5 ft 9 in, minimum and maximum. Standing rigging, the bow thruster, the SD-60 saildrive, the batteries, the bottom paint, the genoa, and the in-mast furling spindle are all dated 2025. The saildrive replaced a Dock & Go system. No survey was read for this page.

Where the pages disagree

Length. The specification says 46 ft 4 in. The class card says 46.32 ft, which is the same length. The short display field says 46. Beam. The specification and the class card say 14 ft 6 in, 14.53 ft. A Denison highlight says 14 ft 0 in. Draft. The equipment list says 5 ft 9 in. The class shallow note is 5.75 ft, which is that draft. The class deep keel is 6.73 ft. The same Denison highlight says 5 ft 0 in. Engine. This boat is listed as a Yanmar 4JH5CE, 54 hp, 382 hours. The class card’s factory engine is a Yanmar 4JH4TE, 75 hp. The exterior paragraph on the listing names “the Sense 50’s spacious cockpit.” This boat is a Sense 46. That sentence is brochure language left in the copy.

What the listing leaves open

No diesel heater and no windvane are listed. No life raft is listed. The solar is two soft panels, with no wattage. The battery-charger line is blank. The Star Link line, spelled that way, is a mount, not a terminal. Holding-tank capacity is not printed. Sail age for the main is not stated. The Rainman is listed as 110 volts.

As listed · years only where the page gives one

A new drive on a 382-hour engine.

2025

The rig

Standing rigging replaced in December 2025. Split backstay. In-mast furling, and the spindle for that furler replaced in 2025. Mast height is listed as 68 ft plus an antenna. Primary winches are Harken 50s, two-speed, self-tailing. Halyard winches are one electric Harken 46 and one manual Harken 46. Six clutches each side. The vang is spelled “Ridged” on the list. A Windex is on the masthead.

382

The Yanmar

4JH5CE, 54 hp, 382 hours, SD-60 saildrive fitted in 2025 in place of Dock & Go. Bow thruster replaced in December 2025. Dual helm wheels. A photograph shows the engine under a hatch. It does not show the hour meter. The class card’s 75 hp engine is the other engine.

210

The Onan

6MDKBJ-1100C, 6 kW, 210 hours. Chilled-water air conditioning, three handlers forward, each with its own thermostat. The Rainman is 110 volts, so a passage that wants water wants this generator, or shore power, until someone shows otherwise.

Soft

Solar, water, heat, vane

Two large soft panels under the boom. No watts. A Victron 2,000 watt inverter. Ten AGM batteries from 2025. A Rainman at 26 to 37 gallons per hour. No diesel heater. No windvane. The autopilot is named Raymarine and is not described as tested.

5 ft 9 in

The keel

The equipment list repeats 5 ft 9 in. That matches the class shallow note, 5.75 ft, and not the class deep keel, 6.73 ft. The class hull is a fin with a bulb and dual rudders. This listing describes the draft and the dual wheels. It does not describe the rudders. Some anchorages open up at 5 ft 9 in. The mast at 68 ft plus an antenna does not fit an ordinary 65 ft fixed bridge.

2020

What is already aboard

Zodiac Cadet 270 from 2020, 8 ft 10 in, and a Tohatsu 9.8 from 2021. Bimini and dodger, cockpit partly enclosed. Cockpit shower. Swim platform. Windlass with a remote. The anchor itself is “on the bow,” with no weight. Jabsco electric heads, salt or fresh flush. Axiom Pro 9 inch from 2024, plus a separate Garmin plotter. Fusion stereo, Bose speakers, a Roku television.

  • Fuel 106 gallons, the same figure on the listing and the class card. Water 182 gallons, the same agreement. Holding capacity is not printed.
  • Bottom painted in 2025. Canvas is a bimini and a dodger with a partly enclosed cockpit. Covers for the companionway, the winches, and the helms.
  • The electrical list says 12 volts, 110 volts, shore power at 110, and an alternator. The charger line has no make after the colon.
  • Interior: wood-veneer sole, cloth cushions, five opening hatches, a gimbaled stove and oven, refrigeration and a freezer to port, a microwave, a double sink, pressure water, a filtration spigot, and a 110-volt water heater with an engine heat exchanger.

The route

She can be walked in Key West. The islands are a delivery away.

Phase 2 on the home page wants a boat already in the Northwest. Second Wind is the southern case that page already allows. Walk her in Key West. The San Juan year is then a delivery north, or the route starts in Florida and the islands are skipped. The delivery line in the calculator is zero until a number exists. Nothing on the listing quotes one.

What is already paid for is real: rigging, spindle, saildrive, thruster, batteries, bottom paint, and a genoa, all in 2025, on an engine with 382 hours and a generator with 210. A watermaker, an inverter, a dinghy, and some solar are aboard. What the route still has to buy, or prove, is heat, a second way to steer, a raft, a solar wattage, and enough fuel. One hundred six gallons is the listing tank. The comfort number is 26. The capsize screen is 1.97. Those are the class card’s figures, and they are the light end of this study.
