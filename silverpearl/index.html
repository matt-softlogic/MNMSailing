The boat

Same-level glass doors, and a horizon from the saloon.

Silver Pearl is a 2011 Moody Decksaloon 45, drawn by Dixon Yacht Design with interior by Design Unlimited, built by Moody (Hanse group). Berthon sold her new in June 2012 to her current owners. She was based in the Chesapeake, with summer cruises to Maine and one winter in Florida and the Bahamas.

The saloon, the cockpit and the galley sit on one level behind sliding glass doors. That is the deck-saloon brief, done properly.

Three cabins, two heads, both with stall showers and electric toilets. Owner's cabin forward with a centerline double. Satin mahogany joinery and synthetic acacia soles. The forward-facing chart table on the port side carries engine controls, an autopilot control and a Raymarine plotter. There is no inside wheel. Inside steering is through the pilot.

Electric in-mast furling North Norlam main, self-tacking furling jib, electric winches, bow and stern thrusters, twin wheels, retractable bimini, a fold-down swim platform over a dinghy garage, retractable anchor arm, high bulwarks, full air conditioning, and Raymarine electronics about three years old.

What fits the brief

A real deck saloon, one level from cockpit to galley. 45 ft, inside the 44 to 56 ft window. Fixed keel at 6 ft 8 in, well under 8 ft 6 in. One owner since 2012. Easy short-handed sail plan. The photos also show a Fischer Panda generator and a Victron MultiPlus Compact.

What the listing leaves open

No diesel heat, watermaker or solar is listed, and no rigging date. That is zero of the four systems the brief asks for. She is an East Coast boat, so getting her west is a truck or a ship. Glued teak decks. Engine hours with no service records seen.

Red flags

What has to come back boring.

Teak

Glued teak decks, 15 years old

The photos show the teak weathered grey, with the black seams mostly tight in the shots we have. Thickness, lifting seams and the core under it are survey items. A re-deck is a big job.

~2,200 h

Volvo D3-110. No service history has been seen. Oil analysis, a log, and a look at the saildrive or shaft before anything else.
