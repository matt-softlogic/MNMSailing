The boat

A blue-water Frers hull, refitted underneath.

Unicorn is a 1988 Hylas 44, German Frers design, built by Queen Long in Taiwan. The prior listing calls her a MKII. She is a center-cockpit cutter with a three-spreader keel-stepped mast, a long fin keel and a skeg-hung rudder. Her Flag Blue hull is almost certainly painted. The paint system and its age are unknown. California Yacht Sales lists her in San Diego at $144,500, and says she has cruised Mexico, Baja and Southern California.

She has the most systems already aboard of any boat in the under-$150,000 search: lithium, solar, a generator, modern Raymarine glass, and a 2024 drivetrain.

The engine is a Yanmar 4JH3E, 55 hp, at 2,195 hours. The 4JH3E is a later model than the hull, so she has been repowered. The repower date is unknown, and the prior listing's "1987" engine year cannot be right. In 2024 the transmission and clutch were rebuilt, and she got a new prop shaft, coupler, cutless bearing, Tides Marine dripless seal and exhaust elbow. Since 2023 she has had a Flexofold three-blade, new fuel lines, a Vetus strainer and a custom fuel-polishing system.

Below: an aft owner's cabin with a centerline island berth and an ensuite with a separate shower, a forward cabin with its own head and shower, a U-dinette and settee, heavy teak joinery and a teak-and-holly sole. Water 150 gal, fuel 110 gal, holding 40 gal. The prior listing says three stainless fuel tanks and four stainless water tanks.

What is already paid for

Two lithium house batteries, 600 Ah, 2020. Two 300 W Kyocera panels on the stern arch, 600 W, with a Blue Sky controller. Fischer Panda 8 kW generator, about 1,491 hours. Mastervolt ChargeMaster 12/50-3 and a Victron BMV-712. Raymarine displays at the helm and the nav station, radar, AIS and autopilot. Rocna 25 kg on 190 ft of 3/8 in chain, Lewmar windlass motor 2024. Doyle StackPack. Electric Harken winch. Frigoboat with a 2025 compressor. Jabsco heads 2024/25. Water heater 2023. Bottom paint 2023.

What the listing leaves open

Standing rigging is "not original, unclear when last replaced, 2023 inspected." The chainplates are the Hylas 44's encapsulated stainless weldament, and you cannot see them without taking cabinetry off. No life raft, no wind vane, and no jacklines are listed. The watermaker is only partly installed. There is no diesel heat, only Cruisair reverse-cycle. Sail ages are not given. The AC/DC panel looks original. The draft is 6.8 ft on this listing and 6 ft 0 in on the last one.

All-in · San Diego start into Mexico · estimates

What she costs to get to Mexico, and then across the Pacific.

The plan is to start from San Diego, with no transport. All-in is the purchase, plus the refit, plus 15% contingency. The low end of each range assumes California's removal exclusion applies: buy, leave directly for Mexico, and stay out of California for 12 months, documented. Then California use tax is $0. The high end includes California use tax at 7.75%, which applies if she is commissioned, refitted or used in California. The $130,000 figure is a planning purchase, the same idea as Paragon's planning close. It is not an offer, and no broker has agreed to it. This is planning arithmetic, not tax advice.

For California Yacht Sales, before anyone flies

Ask these before a number.

  1. Engine serial number, repower year and installer, invoices and maintenance log. Who rebuilt the transmission in 2024?
  2. Standing rigging: when was it replaced, by whom, and with what terminals? Can we see the 2023 rig inspection?
  3. Chainplates: ever inspected, re-bolted or replaced? Any leaks at the chainplates or the mast partners? Will the seller allow cabinetry panels off for an inspection?
  4. Any prior surveys, purchase or insurance, especially from 2023? Why did the Yachtfinders listing end in March 2026?
  5. Watermaker: make and model, what is missing to finish it, and are the parts aboard?
  6. Life raft: is one aboard? EPIRB make, battery date and registration? PLBs, jacklines and tethers?
  7. Do the Starlink Roam and Lokket, the Achilles dinghy with the Yamaha 20 hp, and the spares convey?
  8. Keel: deep or shoal, 6 ft 0 in or 6.8 ft? Any keel-seam weeping or keel-bolt work?
  9. The three stainless fuel and four stainless water tanks: condition, any replacements? Fuel-polishing details?
  10. Electronics: exact display models (Axiom or eS), radar, autopilot computer and Octopus drive, and their ages. Is there an inverter?
  11. Sails: makers and ages of the main, yankee, staysail and spinnaker.
  12. Hull paint: system and year, any blistering. Bottom paint 2023: when is the next haul?
  13. Deck core: any known moisture or recored areas? Hatch and portlight rebedding history?
  14. Mexico and Baja: dates, routes, any offshore passages. How long have the current owners had her? Any charter history?
  15. USCG documentation number and HIN. Any liens?
  16. The full equipment list "available on request."

How to read her

Unicorn makes sense only as a San Diego start into Mexico. Her strengths are already aboard: power, electronics, the drivetrain, and an engine with sensible hours. Her money goes into the rig, the chainplates, a raft, a vane and the gear to go offshore. A clean chainplate inspection and a rig answer decide whether she stays on the list.

Seeing her means a flight to San Diego. Get the documents and the broker's answers first.
